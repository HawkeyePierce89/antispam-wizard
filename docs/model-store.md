# ASW_Model_Store

Reference moved out of `CLAUDE.md`. The lazy per-writing-system model loader: unit layout, packed representation, compressed envelope, learned-sample budget, admission control, erasure purge, and every test that pins each rule. See also `docs/data-storage.md` for the cache layout, fingerprints and measurements.

`ASW_Model_Store` — **lazy per-writing-system model loader**, constructed by `build_checks()` from `( ASW_Tokenizer, array $excluded_tokens, ASW_Training_Data, ?ASW_Sample_Repository )` and handed to the Bayesian and Similarity checks. It replaced the single monolithic classifier transient: `build_checks()` now reads **no** corpus file and **no** transient, and a submission loads the unit for the script it detects and nothing else. `script_for( $text )` detects once per text (memoised by `md5( $text )`) so both checks share one detection pass; `script_classifier()`, `script_sample_tokens()`, `combined_classifier()`, `combined_sample_tokens()` and `combined_has_script_samples()` each load their unit on first ask and memoise it for the request. Verdicts are byte-identical to the old eager path because the resolution rules moved unchanged into the two checks (per-script classifier only when **both** its doc counts reach `MIN_TRAINING_SAMPLES`, combined otherwise; the script's sample tokens when that list is non-empty, combined otherwise; and the legacy `tokenize()` mode only when `combined_has_script_samples()` is false, which reproduces the old `empty( $script_sample_tokens )` test — the combined unit is the only place that answer can come from, which is also the only case in which it is loaded), and the combined unit's **classifier** is trained in the old order (legacy `data/{spam,ham}-samples.txt`, then each script in `SCRIPT_GROUPS` order, then the repository's `unknown`-script rows). The combined unit's **similarity corpus** is the one deliberate exception, and an accepted deviation rather than an oversight: it holds the legacy file's token lists plus as much of the repository's `unknown`-script rows as `LEARNED_SIMILARITY_BUDGET` pays for (below), never the ten per-script lists, and they are not composed at read time either. `ASW_Similarity_Check::resolve_from_store()` consults that corpus whenever the detected script's own list is empty — so the deviation applies to every body that falls back, not only to `unknown` ones. On the shipped corpus every named script has a non-empty list, so what it serves is the bodies `ASW_Script_Detector::detect()` routes to `unknown`: one with **no letters at all** (a bare phone number, say), one written wholly in a script outside the ten `SCRIPT_GROUPS` (Armenian, Georgian, Khmer, Lao …), or one whose detection hit a PCRE limit. A **named** script lands there too when its own list comes out empty. That list is built from the script's **spam** samples alone (`build_script_unit()` tokenises `$spam` and nothing else), so a populated ham corpus does not keep a script off this path: it is empty when the script's **spam** corpus file is missing, unreadable or empty with no learned spam samples under that script, or when every spam sample of it tokenises to nothing under the site's excluded tokens — and loses little by it for the same reason one step removed: that script's own samples are exactly the ones the fallback found missing, so they were absent from the monolithic combined corpus as well, and what the body loses is the *other* nine scripts' lists, other writing systems at low cosine against it except through the tokens they share (the phone marker, numerals, embedded Latin) (`SimilarityCheckTest::test_store_backed_check_falls_back_to_the_combined_unit_for_a_script_without_samples` and `…test_the_combined_unit_scores_a_fallback_body_against_the_legacy_corpus` pin that path through a fixture whose Latin corpus is empty). How far the score moves depends on the route, and the four are not one case. **Small** for two: cosine similarity of a **letter-less** body against word samples is near zero except through `ASW_Tokenizer::PHONE_TOKEN` and bare numerals, and that marker is already the strongest single feature of the combined Bayesian classifier, which stays trained on every script's samples and cached in full; and a **named script whose list came out empty** loses only the other nine, as above. Little, **not nothing**: a body that does share those tokens scores lower than it did and can fall below the threshold it used to clear, which is why this is recorded as a verdict change rather than a no-op. **Potentially substantial** for the other two, and stated as such rather than folded into that argument: an **unsupported-script** body matches the ten lists near zero only as far as the shipped corpus *files* go — a learned sample is filed under its comment's *dominant* script and `ASW_Tokenizer::tokenize_for_script()` picks a tokenisation strategy without filtering tokens by script, so a mostly-Latin comment quoting Armenian sits in the `latin` list carrying its Armenian tokens, and where that has happened the removed list could have matched at full strength; and a body routed to `unknown` by a **PCRE failure** in `ASW_Script_Detector::detect()` is ordinary text in one of the ten scripts whose own list is precisely the one the combined unit no longer carries — a strong match before, near zero after, for any of the eight word-tokenised scripts (CJK and Thai lose little, since `unknown` word-tokenises where those lists hold bigrams). Both keep the combined classifier, so the Bayesian verdict is untouched; the Similarity verdict for them can be lost outright. It **is** a verdict change for every body that falls back to that corpus, and it is what brings the combined unit from 1 859 022 bytes to 766 696 under the object cache's 1 048 576-byte per-item limit: no representation gets it under while it still carries all ten lists, which alone are 1 101 154 bytes packed. `combined_has_script_samples()` does **not** move with the corpus — it is still computed at build time from the loaded per-script samples (`any_script_sample_tokenises()`, short-circuiting at the first non-empty tokenisation) and stored as the boolean it already was, so it cannot flip to false merely because the lists are no longer stored; that boolean decides the Similarity check's legacy `tokenize()` mode, and a flip would change tokenisation for every fallback body. `ModelStoreTest::test_the_combined_similarity_corpus_holds_only_the_legacy_and_unknown_script_lists`, `…test_the_combined_unit_still_reports_script_samples_it_no_longer_stores`, `…test_the_combined_classifier_still_trains_on_every_scripts_samples` and `…test_a_cyrillic_bodys_similarity_corpus_and_score_are_unchanged` pin all four halves. Per-unit load sequence: compute the unit's **fingerprint** → `get_transient()` on the unit's fixed key → check the fingerprint stored inside the value, then validate the payload → on a hit restore and memoise → on a stale, missing or invalid hit `delete_transient()`, load that unit's samples, `clearstatcache( true )`, recompute the fingerprint, rebuild, and `set_transient( …, DAY_IN_SECONDS )` **unless the fingerprint moved, or a sample read failed while the unit was being loaded** — the two never-cache rules left — and then, **after** the write, `clearstatcache( true )` and read the fingerprint once more, `delete_transient()`ing the row when it has moved. That last step is what makes the cache durable against an erasure: `ASW_Privacy::erase()` deletes the sample rows and *then* purges every unit key, so an erasure landing in the window between the pre-write check and the `set_transient()` was otherwise followed by this request writing its already-loaded, pre-erasure tokens straight back into the row the eraser had just emptied — erased personal data restored after the request reported success, readable for the rest of the unit's day, with nothing left to purge it. The post-write read closes it in both orders (an erasure that already committed has moved the fingerprint, so the payload just written goes; one that commits later purges the row itself) and costs one extra fingerprint read on the cold path only. That drop is **verified rather than fired and forgotten** (`drop_superseded_unit()`): the erasure is over, so a `delete_transient()` the backend refused would leave the pre-erasure tokens this request just wrote as the only copy with nothing left to remove them — the row is read back through the same `stored_unit()` and, when it survived carrying `samples` or `classifier` state, overwritten with a value carrying neither, which this request can do because its own `set_transient()` has just landed (a value with no `fingerprint` member is rebuilt on the next read rather than served). A blanking write the backend refuses as well leaves one lever that is not a write through the options API at all — `delete_unit_rows()`, a prepared `DELETE` of the unit's own `_transient_…`/`_transient_timeout_…` rows (an unwildcarded `LIKE` over `esc_like()`d names, followed by the `wp_cache_delete()`s core's own `delete_option()` makes), skipped under an external object cache where no such row exists and nothing is masked by it because the row it names has already been read back as surviving and is read back again after. A unit that survives *that* is **logged** and nothing more — there is no durable pending-purge record, for the reason given below, and the next erasure request purges every key again in any case. `…test_a_superseded_unit_the_backend_would_not_drop_is_stripped_of_its_samples`, `…test_a_superseded_unit_the_options_api_would_not_drop_has_its_rows_deleted` and `…test_a_superseded_unit_that_survives_every_lever_is_logged` pin the three (`ModelStoreTest::test_a_unit_superseded_between_the_check_and_the_write_is_dropped` and its `combined` twin pin it, through the `$wp_on_set_transient` stub seam that fires just *before* a write lands). A per-script unit whose cached `classifier`/`samples` members are missing, or whose classifier state `set_state()` rejects, is deleted and rebuilt; a per-script classifier needs only **one** doc-count category (a script's corpus may legitimately be one-sided, and requiring both would rebuild it on every request), while the combined unit keeps the stricter existing rule and requires both — a partial combined model would report "insufficient training data" and disable the Bayesian check until the transient expired. `script_unit()` returns null for anything that is not a key of `ASW_Script_Detector::SCRIPT_GROUPS`, so an `unknown`-script body goes straight to the combined unit. A unit is cached in a **packed** representation, the first of the four measures that keep every unit inside the object cache's 1 048 576-byte per-item limit (the other three — the compressed envelope, the learned-sample budget and the prune-until-it-fits admission control — are below): each sample's token list is stored as one space-delimited string (`pack_sample_tokens()`), and each classifier category's `token_counts` map as one interleaved `"token count token count …"` string (`pack_classifier_state()`), while `token_totals` and `doc_counts` — one entry per category — stay native arrays. A space is an **exact** delimiter, not a convenient one: `ASW_Tokenizer` splits the word path on `/[^\p{L}\p{N}\p{M}]+/u` and strips the same class before emitting bigrams on the n-gram path, so no token it emits can contain a space, and `explode( ' ', … )` is the exact inverse of `implode( ' ', … )`; `token_counts` keys that look numeric are cast to `int` by PHP both before packing and after unpacking, so that round trip is identity too. `ASW_Naive_Bayes`'s **state contract** is untouched by any of this — the store unpacks before calling `set_state()`, so the state that class accepts is exactly what it always was, and verdicts, probabilities and similarity scores are unchanged for every script that has a unit. (`classify()` itself takes one change, for the prune below: its Laplace denominator is floored at 1.) The cost is paid on the warm path and is reported rather than hidden: **+0.24 ms per submission** (2.44 → 2.68 ms) as first measured, **+0.3 ms** against the re-measured 2.72–2.78 ms warm path quoted in Data storage below — the same regression, measured twice — against −14% on the one-script cold path and −49% on the `unknown`-script one. A unit written **before** this change carries nested arrays under both members, which is simply a shape this version does not recognise: it is deleted and rebuilt like any other unusable value, and there is no migration to perform — a rebuild costs one cold load and overwrites the row. A cached `samples` member is validated to **both** levels, and a failure **invalidates the unit** rather than being filtered out of it: a member that is not a **string**, an **empty string**, and a string carrying an **empty segment** (a leading, trailing or doubled space, which explodes to an empty token) each make `restore_*_unit()` return null, so the row is deleted and rebuilt. `unpack_classifier_state()` rejects the unit on the same terms: a `token_counts` member that is not an array, a category whose value is not a string, an **odd** number of segments (which no packer that always appends a pair can produce, and which would shift every count after the break onto the wrong token), a **non-integer count** segment, which `(int)` would quietly turn into 0 — a token silently dropped out of every probability the category computes — and an **empty token** segment, which an even-length damaged string can carry (a doubled space between a count and the next token) and which would install a `''` key in `token_counts`, inflating the vocabulary size every Laplace-smoothed probability the category serves is computed from. An **empty string** is the one value that is not a failure there: it is what a category with no learned tokens packs to, and it is handled ahead of the pair walk because `explode( ' ', '' )` yields one empty segment rather than none. Outside the packed array there is one more layer, and it is what the object-cache limit actually measures: **under an external object cache** a unit is stored **compressed**, as `array( 'zlib' => gzcompress( serialize( $unit ), 6 ) )`. The envelope is confined to the cache (`compression_applies()` — `zlib_available() && wp_using_ext_object_cache()`), because that is both the only place the per-item limit exists and the only place a deflate stream can be stored at all: without an external cache a transient **is** the `_transient_<name>` option row, a `longtext` on a utf8mb4 connection, and `wpdb::process_fields()` puts every value through `strip_invalid_text()` — a deflate stream is not valid UTF-8, so the write is refused outright with `Could not perform query because it contains invalid data.` An unconditional envelope therefore cached **nothing** on every site without a persistent object cache — the default — and put every submission on the cold path for good, which is the exact failure the envelope exists to prevent, made universal; `dev/smoke.sh` and the unit suite both pass over it, because the `$wpdb` stub models no charset validation and neither harness reads a unit back on a second request. `dev/bench.php` is what caught it: `cached units: 0` with the warm path at 59 ms instead of 2.7. That row has no per-item limit to respect either (a `longtext` holds 4 GB against a combined unit's 765 095 packed bytes), so the plain array costs it nothing. The envelope member (`COMPRESSED_MEMBER = 'zlib'`) is **explicit rather than inferred**, so the stored form can be recognised without guessing which of the two it is — a value carrying the member is this class's compressed envelope, one that does not is either the plain array the option-backed path writes or something this class never wrote. `COMPRESSION_LEVEL = 6` is a measurement, not a default: over the binding worst case (a full `ASW_Naive_Bayes::MAX_DICTIONARY_SIZE` dictionary in **both** categories plus the whole shipped Cyrillic similarity corpus, 2 085 461 bytes raw) level 4 gives 836 889, level 6 gives 771 963 and level 9 gives 755 307 — level 6 buys 64 926 bytes of margin over level 4, 45% of what is left under the limit once the learned-sample budget is spent, for roughly 20 ms more on a cold rebuild that is paid once per unit per day, where level 9 buys 16 656 more for several times that again. Decompression is essentially level-independent (1.2–1.9 ms per unit), so the level is free on the warm path; level 4 is the recorded alternative. `wrap_unit()` writes the plain array wherever `compression_applies()` is false, and returns **null** — cache nothing at all — when `gzcompress()` comes back false on a host where the envelope applies, which makes the caller skip its `set_transient()` the way the mid-load-race and failed-sample-read rules do. Falling back to the plain array there is what must not happen, and it is worse than caching nothing rather than better: `unwrap_for_read()` is strict in both directions, so that host refuses the value on every later read, deletes the row and rebuilds — and the rebuild calls `gzcompress()` again, fails again and writes the same unservable value again, i.e. a cold rebuild on every submission **plus** a wasted write and delete each time, where writing nothing costs the rebuild alone. The refusal is per request, not a latch: the next request retries (`ModelStoreTest::test_a_refused_gzcompress_caches_nothing_rather_than_an_unservable_value` and `…test_a_unit_is_cached_again_once_gzcompress_takes_the_payload` pin both halves, through the `$GLOBALS['asw_gzcompress_fails']` seam `compress_payload()` reads behind the same `ASW_TESTING` gate). Whether this host has zlib is `zlib_available()`, which reads `$GLOBALS['asw_zlib_unavailable']` behind a `defined( 'ASW_TESTING' )` gate: both stored forms have to be pinned against both capabilities, and neither read rule below is reachable otherwise. `ModelStoreTest` drives the cache half through the bootstrap's existing `$wp_using_ext_object_cache` switch — `test_a_unit_is_stored_plain_without_an_external_object_cache` additionally asserts the row serializes to valid UTF-8, which is the property the unconditional envelope broke, and `test_a_compressed_unit_is_rebuilt_without_an_external_object_cache` pins the other direction — and `CorpusQualityTest` turns that switch on for the whole class, since every size it measures is of what a cache would be handed.
The two readers apply **opposite** rules on purpose, because they answer different questions. `unwrap_for_read()` — the **serving** path, used by `script_unit()` and `combined_unit()` — is **strict**: the stored form must match what this host writes, so a compressed value where this host would not compress — a unit written before zlib was compiled out, or one an external object cache still holds while `wp_using_ext_object_cache()` has stopped reporting it — is null *and so is a plain array where this host would compress* (the form the previous release wrote, and the form an object cache installed after the fact strands), and either way the row is deleted and rebuilt. Note that a *compressed* value can never reach a `_transient_…` option row: the envelope applies only under an external object cache, and with one installed `set_transient()` writes no option row at all. Serving asks "can this be used?", where anything doubtful costs one cold load to replace, and being strict is what keeps `fingerprint_matches()`, `restore_script_unit()` and `restore_combined_unit()` seeing exactly the array shape they saw before there was an envelope. `holds_training_text()` — the **erasure** path — is **lenient**: a recognised envelope is inflated and judged by the existing `UNIT_TEXT_MEMBERS` rules, and an envelope that will *not* inflate (zlib compiled out after the unit was written, a truncated row) is treated as **holding text**, because an erasure asks "might this still hold the subject's comment?", where the same doubt is the whole reason to blank the row — a payload this host cannot inflate is still the erased comment's token lists, and reading it as text-free would close the erasure over them. Both share one decoder, `inflate_unit()`, so the two can never disagree about what a value holds; its `@gzuncompress()`/`@unserialize()` each carry an annotated `phpcs:ignore WordPress.PHP.NoSilencedErrors.Discouraged` on their own single-line statement, because a truncated row must rebuild rather than append a plugin-attributable warning to the `debug.log` `dev/smoke.sh` scans on every submission that reads it. The inflate runs under a **ceiling** — `gzuncompress( $payload, MAX_INFLATED_UNIT_BYTES )`, 64x the item limit — rather than unbounded: deflate reaches ~1000:1, so a damaged row that is still valid deflate would otherwise allocate hundreds of megabytes and fatal the submission path, the one failure `ASW_Detector::run()`'s fail-open catch cannot absorb. 64x is far past anything this class writes (the best-compressing shipped unit is 5.73x, the worst-case measurement 2.53x), and past the ceiling `gzuncompress()` returns `false`, which both callers already read as “will not inflate”. That ceiling binds the **writer** as well, and is a second axis rather than a restatement of the item limit: the compressed size and the inflated size move independently, so a history of long, distinct, highly repetitive tokens — `ASW_Tokenizer` caps a sample's token *count*, never a token's length, and one 65 536-character comment buys one dictionary entry — serialises far past 64 MiB while deflating to well under 1 048 576. Weighing the compressed value alone accepted such a unit, after which every read refused it, deleted the row and rebuilt — a cold rebuild *plus* a wasted multi-megabyte write on every submission for good, the exact failure admission control exists to prevent. `storable_unit()` therefore prunes against both, through the one `inflate_ceiling()` both sides read (`ModelStoreTest::test_a_unit_that_would_inflate_past_the_readers_ceiling_is_pruned` and `…test_a_unit_the_reader_could_never_decode_is_not_written_at_all` pin the prune and its floor, over `$GLOBALS['asw_max_inflated_unit_bytes']`). `stop_storing_text()` keeps writing `BLANKED_UNIT` **uncompressed**, alone among this class's writes: recognisably a unit and recognisably a text-free one without needing zlib to read it at all. Routed through `wrap_unit()` instead, the blanked value would go in deflated on a compressing host, and a later reader whose zlib had gone would inflate-fail and — by the lenient rule above — report the now-empty unit as still holding the erased comment's text, turning a clean erasure into a permanent `items_retained` / “please retry” loop (`ModelStoreTest::test_the_blanking_write_goes_in_uncompressed_on_a_compressing_host` pins it, and is the only assertion of the rule that can fail — a case that turns zlib off before the write passes whether the envelope is applied or not).
The second half of the bound is `LEARNED_SIMILARITY_BUDGET = 131072` — the packed bytes of *learned* spam a unit's similarity corpus may carry. The shipped corpus is fixed and measured; a site's learned samples are not, and nothing else in the plugin bounds them, so without a cap a unit grows with the site's whole moderation history until it crosses the limit, after which a persistent object cache refuses the write silently and every submission in that writing system pays a cold rebuild for good. The number is what is left once the worst case is paid for: the binding unit compresses to 771 963 bytes as above (combined 680 101, latin 589 456), deflate never expands its input by more than ~0.03% so `B` packed bytes of learned lists add at most `B` compressed even if they do not compress at all, and at `B = 131 072` the binding worst case is 903 035 bytes — 86.1% of the limit, 145 541 bytes of margin (combined 811 173 / 77.4%, latin 720 528 / 68.7%). What 128 KiB buys: ~207 samples of the pessimistic `CorpusQualityTest` shape (40 words of entirely novel vocabulary, 617 packed bytes each) or ~408 typical 300-character comments at ~300 packed bytes, against a shipped Cyrillic corpus of 3 527 spam lists. A sample costs its packed length **plus `LEARNED_SAMPLE_OVERHEAD_BYTES` (16)** for the array element that carries it: counted in token bytes alone the budget bounds the strings and not the array holding them, so the number of admitted lists is capped only by how short a sample can tokenise to — a history of three-byte tokenisations admits some 43 000 lists the budget scores at 131 072 bytes and that serialise to roughly 775 000, which on the cache-backed host *without* zlib (where the plain packed array is what the item limit weighs) is enough for the samples alone to cross it, and nothing can prune them back, since `storable_unit()` spends vocabulary and not corpus. Charging the overhead bounds the admitted corpus in serialised bytes rather than only in token bytes — at most `LEARNED_SIMILARITY_BUDGET / 16` lists, whatever they hold — and costs a realistic corpus 5% of the budget (`ModelStoreTest::test_the_budget_bounds_what_a_corpus_serializes_to_not_only_its_tokens` pins it). `admit_within_budget( $learned, $script )` spends it from the **newest** sample downwards — `ASW_Sample_Repository::find_all()` orders by ascending `id`, which is the only ordering that survives content dedup and re-insertion (`created_at` is not) — and **stops at the first sample whose packed length would take the running total past the budget, admitting nothing older**. It does not skip that sample and reach further back for a smaller one: the corpus is meant to be the site's *recent* spam, and one that silently reached past a large sample would be neither bounded by recency nor easy to reason about. A sample that tokenises to nothing costs no budget and is passed over without stopping the walk. A sample is therefore admitted **whole or not at all**, and can never be over the budget on realistic text (a comment is bounded by `ASW_Detector::MAX_COMMENT_LENGTH` and a tokenisation by `ASW_Tokenizer`'s 10 000-token cap, ~110 000 packed bytes for real text); a pathological one that is over it stops the walk and the state self-heals, since the next sample the site learns is newer still and the oversized one moves further out of budget rather than blocking a growing tail forever. `build_script_unit()` takes the spam half **split**, so the classifier trains on `file + learned` in the order and over the set `build_checks()` used — byte-identical state — while the similarity corpus takes the shipped lists whole plus only the admitted tail; `build_combined_unit()` puts its `unknown`-script rows through the same walk and never caps the shipped legacy lists. The fingerprints are **untouched**: a sample beyond the cap still moves `script_fingerprint()` / `combined_fingerprint()` and still invalidates the unit, because the classifier depends on it. (`ModelStoreTest::test_the_learned_similarity_corpus_never_exceeds_the_budget`, `…test_the_budget_keeps_the_newest_learned_spam_and_drops_the_oldest`, `…test_a_max_token_sample_is_admitted_whole_and_older_samples_still_fit`, `…test_a_sample_over_the_budget_is_skipped_and_nothing_older_follows_it`, `…test_a_sample_that_tokenises_to_nothing_is_passed_over_without_stopping_the_walk` — the zero-token `continue` is load-bearing in both directions: a `break` there truncates every learned corpus at the first punctuation-only or wholly-excluded body, and appending the empty list instead packs to `''`, which `restore_script_unit()` rejects, so the unit would be deleted and rebuilt on every submission in that script for good —, `…test_the_combined_units_learned_rows_take_the_budget_and_its_legacy_lists_do_not`, `…test_learned_ham_never_enters_a_similarity_corpus` and `…test_a_sample_the_budget_skips_still_invalidates_the_cached_unit` pin the walk, the verdict change and the untouched fingerprints.)
That is a **verdict change**, stated rather than folded away: the Similarity check stops matching a body against learned spam older than the budget's reach for that script. The Bayesian check is untouched **by the budget** — the classifier trains on every learned sample, and its vocabulary is bounded only by `ASW_Naive_Bayes::MAX_DICTIONARY_SIZE` and, under an external object cache, by `storable_unit()`'s prune below. One accepted imprecision follows from that split and is left rather than fixed: the General tab's **Loaded Models** counts are the classifier's view, so once a script's learned spam exceeds the budget they overstate what the Similarity check actually matches against (and, on a host whose unit was pruned, what the Bayesian check does too). Correcting the string would cost a POT regeneration plus `msgmerge`/`msgfmt` across all nine `.po`/`.mo` files for a number that is right for the check it is mostly read about. The third measure is what closes the axis neither of those two can, and it is admission control rather than a budget: **`storable_unit()` weighs the value it is about to write and prunes it until the cache can hold it**. `ASW_Tokenizer` caps a sample at 10 000 tokens but puts no cap on a token's *length*, and `ASW_Naive_Bayes::learn()` admits new tokens first-come-first-served until the *count* cap, so a site's learned history decides the dictionary's bytes: over realistic 4–10-character tokens a full two-category dictionary packs to 1 698 179 bytes and the whole unit compresses to 771 963, but over 10-character CJK tokens — reachable, since a Cyrillic-dominant sample is word-tokenised and keeps a CJK run whole as one 30-byte token — it packs to 3 300 304 and compresses to **2 210 916, 211% of the limit on the classifier alone**, before a single similarity sample. `MAX_CACHED_UNIT_BYTES = 1048576` is therefore measured against rather than predicted: `stored_size()` is `strlen( serialize( … ) )` of the wrapped value — what an object cache backend serializes and weighs itself — and while that is at or over the limit `halve_dictionary()` drops the least frequent half of each category's vocabulary and the value is weighed again. The limit it is weighed against is `MAX_CACHED_UNIT_BYTES` less `CACHE_ITEM_OVERHEAD_BYTES` (1 024): memcached weighs the whole *item* — struct, key, flags suffix and value — where `stored_size()` can only measure the value, so weighing against the raw figure leaves a sliver in which a unit passes here and the backend still refuses it silently, which is the exact failure the mechanism exists to prevent. The halving bottoms out at an **empty** vocabulary — the terminal state the mechanism is written against, since a unit that will not fit even without a dictionary caches nothing — and only then does the walk return null. Stopping a token short instead left a unit that fits only with an empty vocabulary uncached, a band one token wide on ordinary text and a whole comment wide on the long-token history these byte bounds exist for. An emptied category is a valid state only because `ASW_Naive_Bayes::classify()` floors its Laplace denominator at 1: it would otherwise divide by `token_totals + vocabulary_size`, which is then 0 — a `DivisionByZeroError` that `ASW_Detector::run()`'s fail-open catch swallows, disabling the Bayesian check for the unit's whole day while writing a plugin-attributable line into the `debug.log` `dev/smoke.sh` scans on every submission. That floor also closes the pre-existing sibling case (a classifier whose every training sample tokenised to nothing), and `ModelStoreTest::test_the_halving_walk_bottoms_out_at_an_empty_vocabulary` pins it. Frequency is the right thing to spend (a token seen once carries one smoothed likelihood, where the head of the distribution is what a classification turns on), and the dropped occurrences leave `token_totals` with the tokens, exactly as `learn()` counts neither a token it refuses to admit nor its occurrences — so the pruned state reads as a model that never saw them rather than one whose smoothing denominators remember them. Halving converges in at most ~16 rounds from a full dictionary, each round cheaper than the last, and the loop is entered only on a host that reached the limit at all. A unit that will not fit even with an empty dictionary caches nothing — the same answer a refused `gzcompress()` gets — and leaves its live model untouched, since spending vocabulary would buy it nothing there. **The prune is handed back to the live classifier** through `set_state()` before the write, so the request that built the unit classifies with exactly the model it cached; a cache holding a model its own builder did not use would make a verdict depend on whether the unit happened to be warm, which is a far worse bargain than the vocabulary the prune costs. The whole mechanism is gated on `size_limit_applies()` — `wp_using_ext_object_cache()`, and deliberately *not* the zlib half of `compression_applies()`: a host with a cache but no zlib stores the plain packed array and still has the limit to respect, which is what also closes what used to be the second residual here (the 2 085 461-byte raw worst case is now pruned to fit like any other). Without an external cache a transient is a `longtext` option row with no per-item limit, so nothing is weighed and nothing is pruned — pruning a model to fit a bound that does not exist would spend detection quality for nothing. Measuring is what makes it exact, and the compression-independent alternatives were measured before it was chosen: a **token-length cap** would have to sit at or under 12 bytes (the same dictionary stores 1 744 832 / 1 361 368 / 903 976 bytes at 8 / 6 / 4 CJK characters), where only 52% of the shipped corpus's own tokens survive and its longest — a 174-byte Thai run, Thai having no inter-word spaces — is cut by a factor of fourteen; and a **packed-byte budget** on the dictionary has to assume a 1.0x compression ratio, so it prunes ordinary sites whose units compress 2.5–3.5x and are nowhere near the limit — per unit it at least preserves the shipped models, where a single global one would have to hold every classifier under ~533 000 bytes to fit 1 048 576 once the shipped Cyrillic similarity corpus and `LEARNED_SIMILARITY_BUDGET` (~515 000 packed bytes) are paid for, which is less than the shipped combined unit's own classifier. Refusing the write when the wrapped payload is over the limit is **not** a fix and is not done: it is what memcached already does silently, and on a backend without that limit it would be a regression. `ModelStoreTest::test_a_unit_over_the_item_limit_is_pruned_until_the_cache_can_hold_it`, `…test_the_request_that_prunes_a_unit_classifies_with_the_pruned_model`, `…test_a_unit_over_the_limit_is_left_whole_without_an_external_object_cache`, `…test_a_unit_that_cannot_fit_at_all_caches_nothing_and_keeps_its_model` and `…test_the_combined_unit_is_pruned_to_fit_as_well` pin it over a lowered limit — `$GLOBALS['asw_max_cached_unit_bytes']`, read behind the same `defined( 'ASW_TESTING' )` gate as the zlib switches, because reaching the real one takes megabytes of novel vocabulary and a mechanism pinned only by the suite's slowest test is one that stops being run — and `CorpusQualityTest::test_a_dictionary_of_long_tokens_is_pruned_until_the_unit_fits` drives the reachable 2 210 916-byte case through it at the real limit, asserting the premise (unpruned, it is over) as well as the outcome. `ASW_Cosine_Similarity` builds its term-frequency vector by using each token as an array key, so an array or object among them is an illegal offset, i.e. a `TypeError` that `ASW_Detector::run()`'s fail-open catch swallows, silently disabling the Similarity check for the unit's whole 24-hour life while writing a plugin-attributable line into the `debug.log` `dev/smoke.sh` scans on every submission. Filtering the bad lists out instead served a similarity corpus silently short of the samples the unit was built from — in the limit an empty one, which disables the check just as thoroughly — and re-served it from the same corrupted row for a full day, where a rebuild costs one cold load and overwrites it. The empty string is rejected because `build_script_unit()`/`build_combined_unit()` only ever append a non-empty list, so an empty one is not something this class wrote, and a single one is enough to make `ASW_Similarity_Check`'s `! empty( $script_tokens )` test pass and suppress the fall back to the combined corpus a script with no samples is supposed to take. An empty **outer** array stays valid — it is what a script with no spam samples legitimately caches. `ModelStoreTest::test_a_written_unit_stores_each_sample_as_one_delimited_string`, `…test_a_packed_sample_list_round_trips_to_the_freshly_built_tokens`, `…test_a_written_unit_stores_each_categorys_token_counts_as_one_interleaved_string`, `…test_a_packed_classifier_round_trips_to_the_freshly_built_state`, the two `…cached_in_the_old_nested_shape_is_rebuilt` cases and the five malformed-member cases pin the representation and every rejection. The static `unit_keys()` names all eleven units (ten scripts plus combined) and the static `delete_units()` deletes them, prefix-guarded so it can never be handed a transient this class does not own; that pair is what `ASW_Privacy::erase()` uses to purge the cache. `delete_units()` returns **`bool`**, false when a unit is still cached after the purge: a unit the backend refused to drop goes on holding the erased comment's text as a readable token list, which the eraser must report rather than close the request over. It cannot read that off `delete_transient()`, which answers false both for a transient that was not there — the ordinary case, since an erasure purges all eleven keys and a request has usually loaded one — and for a refused delete, so the key is read back and anything other than `false` means it survived (`ModelStoreTest::test_a_refused_delete_whose_text_was_stripped_is_not_reported_as_retained` and `…test_delete_units_reports_success_for_units_that_were_not_cached` pin both sides). That read-back goes through the private `stored_unit()`, **not** `get_transient()`: for an *expired* database-backed transient core deletes both option rows on read and returns false whether or not those deletes landed, so a unit that expired before the erasure arrived would read back as purged while the row — and the erased comment's text in it — stays in `wp_options`; the `_transient_…` option row is therefore consulted directly when the transient API reports a miss (with an external object cache there is no such row and `get_transient()` is the whole truth). `…test_delete_units_sees_an_expired_row_a_refused_cleanup_left_behind` pins it. A purge that did not finish is **not** recorded durably, and deliberately so: the only record the plugin could write goes through the very option store whose refusal is the failure being recorded, and a retry gated on that record is skipped exactly when the record could not be written. `ASW_Privacy::erase()` therefore purges on **page 1 of every erasure request**, whatever that page deleted — which is precisely what the retry its “please retry this erasure request” message asks for looks like, since the sample rows went with the first attempt. The cost is one model-cache flush per erasure request (a rare, admin-approved event) paid by the next comment in each script as a cold rebuild; a later page of the same request leaves the cache alone unless it deleted a sample, so one paginated request flushes once (`PrivacyTest::test_erase_purges_the_model_cache_on_page_one_whatever_it_removed` and `…test_a_retried_erasure_purges_units_the_first_attempt_could_not_drop` pin both). An earlier round of this work kept a pending-purge counter in an option, cleared under an exclusive lock by compare-and-clear; it was removed because the page-1 rule dominates it on every axis — it also survives a refused marker write, a lock that could not be taken, and a web node whose uploads directory is not shared — while a unit that nothing ever comes back for is bounded anyway by its own one-day TTL. What a surviving unit does get is the two-rung ladder `stop_storing_text()` climbs — blank the value, then delete the unit's option rows by statement — each rung verified rather than assumed, since the caller's whole problem is that the obvious write has already been refused. The second rung covers the state Codex's review reproduced, every write through the options API refused at once: without an external object cache a transient **is** those rows, so the `DELETE` removes the erased text even though the transient API and the blanking write both refused (`…test_a_refused_delete_whose_text_was_stripped_is_not_reported_as_retained` and `…test_a_unit_no_write_could_reach_has_its_rows_deleted` pin the two rungs). What `delete_units()` reports is whether **training text is still stored**, not whether every key is gone: a key carrying neither `samples` nor classifier state is no longer personal data, and reporting it as retained would answer an erasure request with a database-error message and a retry request over a unit that holds nothing. That question is answered conservatively for a value that is **not an array**: every value this class writes is one, so a string or object under a unit key is a truncated or otherwise damaged serialization — which `maybe_unserialize()` hands back as the raw string it is, and that string *is* the unit's token lists — so a non-empty non-array value is treated as holding text and put through the same ladder rather than passed over as "not the shape we recognise, therefore harmless" (`ModelStoreTest::test_a_unit_value_that_is_not_an_array_is_stripped_rather_than_trusted` pins it; an empty value carries nothing either way). Being an array is not on its own evidence that the value came from here, so the same rule extends one level in: reading `samples`/`classifier` is sound only for a value carrying the whole shape every write here produces — **both** members of `UNIT_TEXT_MEMBERS` (`classifier`, `samples`), which `export_script_unit()`, `export_combined_unit()` and `BLANKED_UNIT` all set — and an array missing either of them would otherwise be passed over as text-free while holding exactly the tokens the erasure is meant to remove, *and* be excluded from the stray-row sweep as an owned key. That covers `array( 'payload' => array( 'buy', 'pills' ) )` and equally `array( 'fingerprint' => 'abc', 'payload' => array( 'buy', 'pills' ) )`: recognising a value by *any* member this class knows the name of is the permissive reading again one member further in, since a hash or a boolean says nothing about what else the value carries (`…test_a_unit_array_of_an_unknown_shape_is_stripped_rather_than_trusted` and `…test_a_unit_shaped_array_missing_a_text_member_is_stripped_rather_than_trusted` pin the two). A value that *is* recognisable as a unit is judged by those two members alone, extra members and all — `pre_set_transient_*`/`option_*` filters are ordinary WordPress and a member another plugin added is not grounds to flush the unit on every erasure, nor to answer one with a database-error message, since the blanking write carries that member straight back and the unit would then read as retention no lever could clear (`…test_a_unit_carrying_only_members_this_class_writes_is_not_stripped` and `…test_a_text_free_unit_carrying_a_foreign_member_is_not_stripped` pin that side). The blanking write is therefore `BLANKED_UNIT` — `classifier => null`, `samples => array()` — rather than a bare `array()`: recognisably a unit and recognisably a text-free one, and still carrying no `fingerprint`, so the row stays inert as well as empty. A unit that survives even the `DELETE` — a database refusing statements outright — is the one case no lever reaches, so it is **logged**, sanitized and without user input, because no other signal on the site would ever show it; a purge that got the text out logs nothing (`…test_a_unit_that_survives_every_lever_is_reported_and_logged` and `…test_a_purge_that_removed_the_text_logs_nothing` pin both sides). Naming the eleven keys is **not** the whole purge either: a full `delete_units()` also sweeps `_transient_asw_classifiers_%` and `_transient_timeout_asw_classifiers_%` straight out of `wp_options` (`delete_stray_rows()`, one prepared `DELETE … LIKE`), because two kinds of row carry a unit's token lists under a name this version cannot compute — the monolithic `asw_classifiers_<md5>` transient earlier releases wrote, which an upgrade leaves behind, and any unit row stranded when an object cache was dropped into a site that had none (from then on WordPress reads and writes transients in the cache, and core's own `delete_expired_transients()` returns early under an external cache, so nothing ever collects those rows). Which rows the sweep may take depends on where transients live: **without** an external object cache the eleven current keys are excluded by name, since the key loop has already deleted each of them through the transient API and read it back, and a blind `DELETE` would turn a purge the backend refused into one that looks clean; **with** one, no unit is stored in `wp_options` at all, so every matching row is a leftover and all of them go. A `DELETE` the database refused is an unfinished purge like any other. What the sweep cannot reach is a legacy key inside an external object cache — its name is not computable and the cache cannot be enumerated — but that copy expires with its own 24-hour TTL instead of sitting in `wp_options` for good (`…test_delete_units_removes_a_unit_row_no_key_can_name`, `…test_delete_units_removes_unit_rows_an_object_cache_stranded` and `…test_delete_units_reports_a_stray_row_sweep_the_database_refused` pin the three). It works only because a unit's key **names the unit and carries no fingerprint** — `asw_classifiers_cyrillic`, `asw_classifiers_combined` — with the fingerprint of the samples it was built from stored *inside* the value and checked by `fingerprint_matches()` on read (a cached value with no `fingerprint` member is one this class did not write, and is rebuilt rather than served). With the fingerprint in the key, a rebuild wrote a new row **beside** the one it invalidated instead of overwriting it, so every corpus edit and every training write left a full orphaned unit in `wp_options` until its TTL ran out — and an eraser, which can only name keys it can compute, purged the current generation while an older unit went on holding the erased comment's text as a token list. That generation was unreachable even to a caller that took the keys *before* the delete, since the fingerprint had already moved on for an unrelated reason. The key list is now exhaustive and constant, so there is no generation to miss (`PrivacyTest::test_erase_purges_a_unit_cached_before_an_unrelated_training_write` pins it, and fails when the key is fingerprinted again). `tests/ModelStoreTest.php` pins all of it

## Related classes, as documented alongside the store

## `ASW_Tokenizer`

shared tokenizer with word-based tokenization for alphabetic scripts and character n-gram tokenization (bigrams) for CJK/Thai via `tokenize_for_script()`. Both paths first fold every phone-number-like digit sequence (9–15 decimal digits, an optional leading `+`, each pair of digits separated by at most three of space/dot/dash/parentheses, nothing alphanumeric touching either end — `PHONE_PATTERN`) into the letters-only marker `ASW_Tokenizer::PHONE_TOKEN` (`aswphonenumber`), via the public `normalize_phone_numbers()`. A phone number is unique per spammer, so the raw digits are never learnable; the marker turns "this comment carries a phone number" into one feature, which is the strongest single signal of classified-ad spam and what lets the corpus generalise from `пишите 89139991205` to any other number. Accepted boundary: digit groups joined by spaces alone (`2019 2020 2021`, `10 000 000`) are folded too, because `+7 913 999 12 05` is how phones are written; a comma or a word between them keeps them apart (`TokenizerTest::test_space_separated_digit_groups_are_one_phone_number` pins both sides, `test_ordinary_numbers_are_left_alone` pins years, prices and `unique1234`). `ScriptRoutingIntegrationTest::test_unknown_script_does_not_crash` therefore delimits its digits-only body with commas.

## `ASW_Naive_Bayes`

standalone Naive Bayes text classifier (tokenize, learn, classify); supports optional per-script mode for script-aware tokenization. `learn()` and `get_state()`/`set_state()` are unchanged by the model-store work; `classify()` takes one change — its Laplace denominator (`token_totals + vocabulary_size`) is floored at 1, so a state with no learned tokens in a category (every training sample tokenised to nothing, or a unit `ASW_Model_Store::halve_dictionary()` pruned) classifies instead of raising a `DivisionByZeroError` that `ASW_Detector::run()`'s fail-open catch would swallow, silently disabling the check for the unit's whole day while writing a plugin-attributable line into the `debug.log` `dev/smoke.sh` scans (`NaiveBayesTest::test_classify_survives_a_trained_model_with_no_vocabulary` pins it)

## `ASW_Training_Data`

loads seed corpus from `data/*.txt` files and merges user samples from `Sample_Repository`; supports per-script training data (`data/{spam,ham}-samples-{script}.txt`) and combined classifier training. `load_script_samples()` is the merge of two public halves — `load_script_file_samples()` (the shipped `data/*.txt` lines) and `load_learned_script_samples()` (`ASW_Sample_Repository::find_all()`, ascending id, the empty array with no repository) — in the order every caller has always seen, so existing callers are byte-identical. `ASW_Model_Store::script_unit()` calls the two halves rather than the merge, which is what lets it train the classifier on `file + learned` while spending `LEARNED_SIMILARITY_BUDGET` on the learned tail alone, without a second `find_all()`, and what keeps the shipped corpus exempt from the budget by construction. A test double standing in for the corpus must therefore override `load_script_file_samples()`, not `load_script_samples()` (`BayesianCheckTest` and `SimilarityCheckTest`'s doubles do)
